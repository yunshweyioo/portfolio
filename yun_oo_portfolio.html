The Fantastic Four: First Steps
StudioMarvel Studios
TypeFeature Film
Duration~6 months
Team size1 supervisor, 3 leads, 50+ artists, 10+ clients & vendors
Client Relations Stakeholder Management Reporting & Sheets Workflow Design ShotGrid / Flow Team Alignment
Challenge
Notes from every direction — no single version of the truth
Client feedback was coming in through emails, calls, and review sessions — with no central record. Different leads had different information. Artists redid work from conflicting notes. A deliverable nearly went to the client with the wrong version.
→
Action
Built a reporting system that served both the team and the client
Created a client-facing shot tracker — updated before every review — so the client always knew what was approved, in progress, and delivered. Built internal ShotGrid views per lead with shot status, priority flags, and OT risk. Centralized every piece of client feedback and assigned it to the right lead or artist so nothing got missed or acted on twice.
→
Result
Client confidence restored — every pivot absorbed cleanly
When scope shifted mid-production, one update cascaded across every lead — and the client could see the exact impact on timeline, making informed decisions instead of just pushing. A major reprioritization that would have caused chaos went through without missing a step. OT dropped through early blocker flagging.
✦ Key moment When the client made a major mid-show change, there was no scramble — the system made the impact visible immediately and the team pivoted without losing a step.

Challenge
A naming gap costing time in every single meeting
TTF's internal system used numerical sequence codes that didn't match actual sequence names. Every client note had to be looked up before the team could act on it — opening each review with 10+ minutes of translation. On a detail-oriented Apple TV+ show, the stakes were high: shots nearly slipped through unnoticed because the supervisor didn't realize they shared a sequence.
→
Action
Mapped the entire show — independently, in a week
Without being asked, I mapped every sequence number to its name across both the file directory and ShotGrid. Started with the highest-priority sequences and showed the lead and supervisor — they loved it. Completed the full reorganization over a week without disrupting ongoing production. Built a reference system anyone could use, not just me.
→
Result
Cleaner reviews, faster onboarding, a system that held for a year
Reviews moved faster — no time lost translating codes. The supervisor started using the system directly in client calls. New artists onboarded faster. It became the template for how that supervisor runs shows and held up without breaking down for the full year.
✦ Key moment I didn't wait to be asked — spotted a system costing everyone time every day, fixed it myself, and built something durable enough to last the whole show.

About

03

I like the part of production most people dread — the trackers, the schedules, the four hundred moving pieces.

I started on set in Boston, capturing HDRI and photogrammetry data for post — and learned that what I loved most wasn't the camera side. It was the coordination: making sure nothing got lost between set and post, and that everyone knew what was happening next. That instinct took me to The Third Floor, where I spent three and a half years coordinating previs and postvis for Marvel, Apple TV+, Warner Bros., Sony, and Netflix.

My approach is simple: build the system before the chaos arrives. Clean trackers, clear notes, no surprises in reviews. My productions don't miss deadlines — not because nothing goes wrong, but because problems get flagged a day or two before they become emergencies.

I trained as a visual communicator at Syracuse's Newhouse School, and it still shapes how I work — every sheet I build has to be readable at a glance.
